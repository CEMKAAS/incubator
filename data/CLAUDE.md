# `:data` — Room schema and migrations

Split out of the root `CLAUDE.md` so it loads only when working under `data/`.
The rule itself stays in the root file: **no destructive fallback — every schema
change needs a hand-written migration.** What follows is why each existing one
looks the way it does.

## Schema history

- Room DB `incubator_database`, **version 18**, `exportSchema = true` (schemas land in `data/schemas/`). `fallbackToDestructiveMigration()` was **removed on purpose** — with it, every version bump silently wiped users' projects. Migrations are hand-written in `InventoryDatabase`; add a new one for every schema change rather than bumping the version alone. The name and the version are now also `DATABASE_NAME` / `DATABASE_VERSION` in `DatabaseTransfer.kt`, because import has to check an incoming file against them; **`DATABASE_VERSION` must be bumped alongside `@Database(version)`** or import will reject files produced by this very build — `MigrationTest.transferConstantMatchesSchemaVersion` compares the constant against the `user_version` of an opened database, since the annotation itself has binary retention and cannot be read at runtime.
- **`MIGRATION_17_18` puts the breed back into `Batch.Breed` and drops `Batch_breed` and `Batch_candling_breed`.** The rule became «one breed per batch»: a tray of two breeds is laid as two batches (the form does it in one press), each owning its hatch, its cull and its money outright, and the second ledger the two tables kept inside a batch — every batch number held twice and reconciled by `Batch.withBreeds` — had nothing left to do. The column had never been removed (v15 only blanked it), which is what makes this a plain `UPDATE` rather than a table rebuild: each batch gets its rows' names joined with «, » in insertion order (a correlated subquery with `ORDER BY id`, since `group_concat` promises no order) — exactly the string `breedLabel` printed on its card. **A two-breed batch is not split in two by the migration**: its regimen, measurements and candlings are one set, and a second batch carrying copies would be a batch nobody laid; its totals are already the sums over its rows, so no egg or rouble is lost — only the per-breed cut inside such a batch, which the new model does not have. **The joined name is then an ordinary breed**, and shows as one — its own row under the species in «Статистика», its own entry in the dropdown under «Порода» — until the batch is edited by hand; that is what a batch whose breed was typed as «Ломан Браун, Хайсекс» before v15 looked like too, the text field never forbade it, and keeping one of the two names would have lied about what lay in the tray. `Batch_candling.rejected` was likewise the sum of its per-breed rows, so the candling table goes without a back-fill. `MigrationTest.migrate17To18_…` pins the join, the untouched totals and the two dropped tables (`validateDroppedTables = true`).
- **`MIGRATION_16_17` adds `Batch_measurement.groupId`** — a nullable `TEXT`, one plain `ADD COLUMN`, no back-fill: it marks a measurement taken **for the whole incubator** («Внести замер» under the batch list), and nothing recorded before v17 was taken that way, so `NULL` on every old row is the truth rather than a default. A device reading is written as one copy per running batch, into that batch's current-day `Batch_value` row, and the copies share one UUID in this column; the copies themselves are ordinary rows, which is the whole point — the batch sheet, the day analytics, the totals and the averaged schedule for export read them without knowing the device exists. Only the incubator's own sheet reads the column, to fold a group back into one journal line and to edit or delete the group whole. No index: the journal is fetched by `idValue` (the day rows of the running batches) and grouped in memory, and a group edit or delete fetches its copies by the mark (`ItemDao.getMeasurementGroup`, a full scan of a table that answers in milliseconds and is asked once per press) so that a copy left in a batch finished after the reading is reached too. `MigrationTest.migrate16To17_addsGroupId_andKeepsMeasurements` checks the DDL against `17.json`.
- **`MIGRATION_15_16` gives a breed its own cull and its own two prices, and creates `Batch_candling_breed`.** Five ordinary column adds on `Batch_breed` — `rejected`, `price` + `pricePerEgg`, `chickPrice` + `chickPricePerHead`, the same pairs the batch itself carries — because until v16 a breed knew only «заложено» and «выведено», so «which breed cost more and which one dropped out more» had no answer at all, though that is the sort of question a tray gets split for. **The back-fill touches only batches with exactly one breed**, and that is not caution but the only thing knowable here: before v16 every breed row *was* a whole batch (`MIGRATION_14_15` made it out of the `Breed` column), so the batch's cull and prices are its cull and prices. A two-breed batch cannot be split retroactively by anything but invention — and none exists, since the tray became divisible in the same version that added the columns. The `COUNT(*) = 1` guard is left in the SQL as the statement of that assumption. Note `pricePerEgg` is copied from the batch rather than defaulted: «за яйцо» mistaken for «за всё» misprices a clutch by a factor of its egg count, silently. The new table is the per-breed half of a candling's cull: **two foreign keys, both cascading** (its candling and its breed), a unique (`idCandling`, `breedId`) that is the relative of the candling's own unique (`idPT`, `day`), and nothing to migrate into it — culls were recorded as one number per tray, and splitting one afterwards would be invention, so old candlings simply had no rows. Because that table pointed at `Batch_breed.id`, breed ids had to survive a batch edit (the former `ItemDao.updateBatchWithBreeds`). **Both tables are gone since v18** (above); the migration itself stays, tested by `MigrationTest.migrate15To16_movesCullAndMoneyIntoTheSingleBreed` over `15.json`, because a v15 database still has to pass through it.
- **`MIGRATION_14_15` creates `Batch_breed` and moves `Batch.Breed` into it** — one row per breed of a batch, with its eggs and its hatched count, foreign key straight at `Batch` with a cascade and the usual `index_Batch_breed_idPT`. A table rather than a wider column because a tray can hold two breeds of one species and each has its own count; a text column could hold one. The copy is one `INSERT … SELECT`: every non-blank `Breed` (trimmed) becomes one row with the batch's own `Egg_all` and `Egg_all_end` — exactly what a single breed was — and a blank or whitespace-only one produces **no row**, since «Без породы» is the absence of a row, not a row with an empty name. Then `UPDATE Batch SET Breed = ''`: the column stays in the schema and in `BatchEntity`, but empty — dropping a column means rebuilding `Batch`, which five child tables reference, and that is not a price worth paying for an unused field; between v15 and v17 the mapper wrote `""` into it and nothing read it — the rows were the only answer. **That is exactly what made v18 cheap**: the column was still there to be read again. The DDL is copied from `15.json`. Tested by `MigrationTest.migrate14To15_movesBreedIntoRows_andBlanksTheColumn` over `14.json`.
- **`MIGRATION_13_14` indexes the five foreign keys** — `Batch.incubatorId`, `Batch_value.idPT`, `Batch_measurement.idValue`, `Batch_time.idPT`, `Batch_species.idPT`. Until v14 the database had exactly three indexes and all three were unique ones, put there so that a duplicate could not exist; not one existed for the sake of a lookup. The cascade paid the most for that: deleting a batch made SQLite scan `Batch_measurement` whole for each of the 17–31 `Batch_value` rows being removed, and deleting an incubator multiplied it by the number of its batches. Plain `CREATE INDEX IF NOT EXISTS`, no table rebuild — nothing to copy and nothing to roll back. **The names must be the ones Room generates** (`index_<table>_<column>`): the schema check compares them at open time, and a name of your own would fail it. This is also the first migration in the project to have a test — `data/src/androidTest`, `MigrationTestHelper` over `13.json` — and the shape of it is the template for closing the other twelve. The first one cannot be tested that way at all: `exportSchema` was switched on after it, so there is no `1.json` to create a v1 database from.
- **`MIGRATION_12_13` creates `Custom_species` and `Custom_species_day`** — свои виды птицы, the regimens a user writes themselves. Two tables rather than columns, because a species has as many days as its owner described and the incubation term *is* their count; one row per day is exactly what `Batch_value` already does for a batch's schedule, and the four plan values are named and typed the same on purpose — the same quantity at two stages of its life, with the same meaning of `null` («нормы нет»). The day's foreign key points **straight at the species**, since a day means nothing on its own and deleting a species must take its days with it; the unique index on (`speciesId`, `day`) is the one `Batch_candling` has on (`idPT`, `day`), and for the same reason — a second regimen for one day would leave nobody able to say which is right. **The unique index on `Name` is an integrity condition, not tidiness**: a batch references a species by name (`Batch.Type`, and the chips in `Batch_species`), exactly as it references a built-in one, so two species sharing a name would make «whose regimen produced this batch» unanswerable — and that is also why renaming a species walks the batches (`ItemDao.saveCustomSpecies`, one transaction with the days). Nothing to back-fill: custom species did not exist before v13, and the built-in ones still live in code in `domain.incubation` — the table does not replace them.
- **`MIGRATION_11_12` adds `Incubator.Hidden`** — «убран в архив», the device's counterpart of `Batch.Hidden` from v8, and deliberately the same column name: the question is the same one, and a second word for it would only invite the guess that it means something else. An ordinary column add, back-filled to `0` — nothing could be archived before v12, so no device disappears from the start screen because of an update. **There is no `Incubator.Archive` beside it and there will not be**: `Batch` needs two flags because incubation ends and the list membership is a separate matter, while a device never ends — «убран с глаз» is all there is to say about where it belongs.
- **`MIGRATION_10_11` creates `User`** — one row, one column, the owner's name. An ordinary `CREATE TABLE`: nothing to back-fill, since the app never knew a user before. The row is **not** inserted here either — an empty table honestly means «never asked», while an empty name in it would mean «asked, and they left it blank»; `OfflineItemsRepository.getUser()` collapses both into `User("")` for the screen, but that is no reason to invent a row. The DDL is copied from what Room generates: the key is declared without `autoGenerate`, so it lands in a trailing `PRIMARY KEY(...)` rather than as `INTEGER PRIMARY KEY AUTOINCREMENT` on the column. Verified against `data/schemas/…/11.json`, which is the authority for this.
- **`MIGRATION_9_10` adds `Batch.Time` and `Batch.Egg_rejected`** — the hour a batch was laid, and a cull count typed by hand. An ordinary column add. **The time is its own column rather than an appendix to `Date`**: that string is sliced apart both in SQL (`getAllIncubator` sorts by re-cutting it into ISO order) and in the UI (the current incubation day is a diff of two parsed `Date`s), and five extra characters would break every one of those readers. It back-fills to `''`, not `'00:00'` — nobody was asked, and midnight would be an invention; the UI prints an em dash for a blank. `Egg_rejected` back-fills to `0` and does **not** duplicate `Batch_candling`: that table holds what each candling removed, this column holds what disappeared between them (cracked, rotten, dropped). `BatchDetailViewModel.rejectedTotal` adds the two, so «Осталось» subtracts both — which also means the same egg must never be entered in both places.
- **`MIGRATION_8_9` turns turning and airing into numbers, and splits airing into two columns** — `over`, `airingCount`, `airingTime` (INTEGER, nullable) replace `over` + `airing` (TEXT) in both `Batch_value` and `Batch_measurement`. As text they could not be counted: the plan held «2-3», «2 раза по 5 минут», «Авто», and the UI dug the leading number out with a regex — which for «2 раза по 5 минут» found the times and lost the minutes; the measurement used the same column for something else again, the duration of a single airing, so «twice for five minutes» could not be recorded at all. Conversion: «Авто» → `NULL` (**not** `0` — zero means «do not do it», `NULL` means «no quota, the device handles it», the same convention `setAutoIncubator` now writes), a turning range → its **upper** bound («2-3» → 3, «4-6» → 6), «2 раза по 5 минут» → 2 and 5 (the leading number and the number after «по»), anything with no digit → `0` in the plan. In measurements an empty string becomes `NULL` rather than zero — there it meant «not recorded» — and a non-empty value with no digits becomes `1`, since the old turn toggle wrote exactly that. Table rebuild follows `MIGRATION_4_5` step for step and for the same reason: `Batch_measurement` references `Batch_value`, so the measurements park in a keyless scratch table, the day rows are rebuilt with their ids intact, and the measurements come back with a key onto the new table. `LOWER()` is not used to fold «Авто» — SQLite folds ASCII only — so the check is an explicit `IN` list, which is exhaustive because only the app ever wrote that word.
- **`MIGRATION_7_8` adds `Batch.Hidden`** — «убрана в архив». A separate column rather than a second meaning for `Archive`, for the reason above. Back-filled to `0`, i.e. visible: nothing could be hidden before v8, so every existing batch stays exactly where its owner left it.
- **`MIGRATION_6_7` is an ordinary column add** — `Batch.EndReason / ChickPrice / ChickPricePerHead`, the three things the finish dialogs ask for. `ChickPricePerHead` back-fills to `1`, exactly as `PricePerEgg` did in v3: no existing batch has a chick price at all, and «за птенца» is where the dialog starts. `EndReason` cannot be back-filled at all — nobody asked batches finished before v7 why they ended, so an empty reason there honestly means «unknown», not «ran its term».
- **`MIGRATION_5_6` adds `Batch_candling`** — how many eggs were culled at each candling. A separate table rather than a column on `Batch`, because a batch has two or three candlings and one column would keep only their sum, losing which candling removed what. **Its foreign key points straight at `Batch`, not at `Batch_value`** — unlike a measurement, a cull is an event of the batch itself, its total is read where the remaining eggs are counted, and routing it through the day row would buy nothing; the incubation day sits here as a plain column. That column pairs with `idPT` in a **unique index** (`index_Batch_candling_idPT_day`), so a second record for the same day overwrites the first instead of double-counting — `ItemDao.insertCandling` is `OnConflictStrategy.REPLACE` for exactly that. Nothing to back-fill: culling was recorded nowhere before v6, which is why «Осталось» used to equal «Заложено».
- **`MIGRATION_4_5` turns `temp` and `damp` into `REAL`** in both `Batch_value` (the plan) and `Batch_measurement` (the fact). They could never really be text: the app subtracts one from the other to show a deviation, and «60» minus «55» was being parsed out of a string on every recomposition. SQLite cannot change a column's type in place, so both tables are recreated and copied — **and the order is load-bearing**: `Batch_measurement` references `Batch_value`, so the parent cannot be dropped while a child points at it. Hence three steps: measurements move to a scratch table **without** the foreign key, the day rows are rebuilt (ids copied verbatim, which is what keeps the measurement links valid), then measurements come back with a key onto the new table. Conversion is `CASE WHEN REPLACE(TRIM(col), ',', '.') GLOB '*[0-9]*' THEN CAST(… AS REAL) ELSE NULL END` — commas become dots (they are easy to type on a numeric keypad and the quail regimen shipped one), text with no digit at all becomes `NULL` rather than `0.0`, and a range like «37-38» collapses to 37, which is a real loss that a `REAL` column cannot avoid.
- **`MIGRATION_3_4` adds `Batch_measurement`** — the actual thermometer/hygrometer readings the batch sheet records. A separate table rather than columns on `Batch_value`: a day of incubation can hold any number of readings, while `Batch_value` holds exactly one row per day and that row is the *plan*. **Its foreign key points at `Batch_value.id`, not at `Batch` plus a date** — the start date of a batch gets corrected and the phone's clock gets changed, and a reading keyed by calendar date would then sit on the wrong day of incubation. Editing a batch never recreates its `Batch_value` rows (`AddBatchViewModel.save()` only updates the batch and rewrites reminders), so those ids live as long as the batch does. The cascade reaches measurements in two steps: `Batch` → `Batch_value` → `Batch_measurement`. Nothing to back-fill — measurements did not exist before v4.
- **`MIGRATION_2_3` is an ordinary column add** — `Batch.Breed / Price / PricePerEgg` and `Batch_time.note`, the three things the batch form's mockup asks for. `PricePerEgg` back-fills to `1`, not `0`: existing batches have no price at all, and when one is finally typed in, «за яйцо» is the mode the mockup defaults to.
- **`MIGRATION_1_2` is a table restructure, not a column add**, and its ordering is load-bearing: the name `Incubator` is reused for the device, so the old table is first renamed to `Batch_tmp`, then the device table is created and one row synthesized, then `Batch` is created with its FK and filled, then the child tables, then the old ones dropped. The synthesized incubator gets `Capacity = 0` (v1 had no such data — the card asks the user to fill it, editable via the gear icon on the batches screen) while its auto-turn / auto-airing flags are derived with `MAX(CASE WHEN Overturn = 'true' …)` over the existing batches.
- **Child tables are recreated and copied, never renamed.** `minSdk = 26` means SQLite 3.18, where `ALTER TABLE … RENAME TO` does *not* rewrite `REFERENCES` in other tables — that only arrived in 3.25 (API 29). Any future table rename must use the same create-copy-drop dance.
- Entities sit apart from the domain models, so editing a `*Entity` class *is* editing the schema. Two checks, in order of strength: Room stamps an identity hash into the generated `data/build/generated/ksp/debug/kotlin/.../InventoryDatabase_Impl.kt` (`RoomOpenDelegate(2, "<hash>", ...)`) and writes it into `room_master_table` on the device, so pulling the DB and comparing the two proves the migration produced exactly the expected schema; if a refactor is meant to be schema-neutral, that hash must not move; and the generated `CREATE TABLE` there is the authority your migration DDL must match character for character.
- `Migration` overrides `migrate(db: SupportSQLiteDatabase)`. That is the right overload here: `getDatabase` builds Room without a custom `SQLiteDriver`, and the base `migrate(SQLiteConnection)` delegates to the SupportSQLite one in exactly that case.
- Adding a `NOT NULL` column via `ALTER TABLE … DEFAULT …` is safe even though the entity declares no `defaultValue`: Room only compares defaults when the entity itself defines one (`TableInfo.Column.equalsCommon`).

## Moving the whole database in and out

`DatabaseTransfer.kt` is the «Управление данными» section of the settings screen: `exportDatabase(context, out)` and `importDatabase(context, input)`. It lives here rather than in `:app` for the same reason `provideItemsRepository` does — the file name, the schema version and the open Room connection are `:data`'s business, and `:app` cannot see any of them.

- **The SQLite file itself is transferred, not a JSON dump.** Eight tables tied by foreign keys with cascades would need a serializer kept in step with the schema by hand, on every migration, forever; the file is complete by definition. The price is that the file opens in nothing but this app and is tied to a schema version — hence the two checks below.
- **The export is worthless without `PRAGMA wal_checkpoint(FULL)` first.** Room opens the database in WAL mode, so recent writes sit in the sibling `-wal` file, not in the database. A copy taken without the checkpoint is the database as of some earlier moment — missing the latest measurements, possibly the latest batch. The checkpoint runs on Room's own connection (`getDatabase(context).openHelper.writableDatabase`) on purpose: a second connection opened over the file would see its own WAL, not the writer's.
- **The 16-byte SQLite header ends in a NUL, not a space**, and getting that wrong cost the entire import: the constant read `"SQLite format 3 "`, which matches no real file, so every candidate — the app's own export included — was turned away with «это не файл базы данных приложения». One invisible character, invisible in review too; it took running the thing on a device. The constant is now written `"SQLite format 3\u0000"` with an explicit escape rather than a literal NUL in the source.
- **Room's schema-mismatch message never reaches the user.** On a mismatch Room prints both `TableInfo` structures in full, field by field — hundreds of lines that read as a broken app rather than an answer to «почему не взяли мой файл». `openable()` catches the exception and says the file is from another program or a newer build; the detail stays in the log, where the developer is.
- **The only check that answers the real question is opening the candidate with Room itself.** The header, the `Incubator` table and `user_version` are circumstantial: any database with a table of that name passes all three, and what opens the file afterwards is Room, which compares the schema identity hash in `room_master_table` and throws «Room cannot verify the data integrity» when it does not match. An older file instead runs the migrations and breaks on the first `ALTER TABLE`. What that cost: the user's database destroyed, the backup already deleted, the process killed — and the app crashing on every launch afterwards, escapable only by «Очистить данные», i.e. losing everything. So `openable()` copies the candidate next to the working database, opens it through `InventoryDatabase.buildFor(context, fileName)` — same migration list, separate instance, never stored in the singleton — and runs one query, because Room opens lazily and verifies nothing until asked. All of this happens **before** the working database is even closed, so a rejection costs nothing. A bonus: an older file has already been migrated in the candidate, so what lands in place is a file of the current schema.
- **The final step is `renameTo`, not a copy.** `copyTo` starts by truncating the target, and a process killed halfway through would leave a stump where the database was. `rename(2)` within one filesystem replaces the file whole and indivisibly — there is no instant at which the database is absent, whatever interrupts the process. That is also why the candidate lives in the databases directory rather than the cache: rename is only atomic within one filesystem. The copy path survives solely as a fallback for `renameTo` returning false, and keeps its rollback.
- **A failure after the close is its own outcome, not an ordinary rejection.** `ImportResult.RestoredNeedsRestart` exists because «не получилось, ваши данные на месте» is true about the disk and false about the app: `AppDataContainer` takes its `ItemDao` from the Room instance once, lazily, so after `closeAndForget()` every live screen queries a closed database. The user would see a crash right after being told their data was safe and conclude the import ate it. Both that outcome and success therefore offer one button: restart.
- **The swap has a rollback, because it is the one step a failure could not undo.** Copying over a file starts by truncating it: a failure halfway through — out of space, access to the chosen document revoked, the card pulled out — would leave half of someone else's database where the user's used to be. So the working file is set aside whole first (after the checkpoint: a copy whose recent pages are still in `-wal` is no rollback at all) and put back on any error. That is why the failure message ends «Ваши данные на месте.» — it is a promise the code keeps.
- **Import validates, then swaps; it never swaps first.** The incoming stream lands in a cache file, is checked there, and only a file known to be good replaces the working one — a failure at any step before the swap leaves the user's database untouched. Three checks by rising cost: length and the 16-byte `SQLite format 3 ` header (read from the first bytes), then the `Incubator` table (present in *every* schema version, including v1 where it still held batches), then `user_version`. **A file with an older `user_version` is accepted** — Room migrates it on the next open exactly as it would a stale user's database. A newer one is rejected outright: there are no downward migrations and there never will be.
- **`-wal` and `-shm` are deleted along with the old database.** They belong to the file being replaced; SQLite meeting a journal from a different database will either replay foreign pages onto it or refuse to open.
- **The swap runs under Room's own singleton monitor** (`InventoryDatabase.exclusively`). Between `closeAndForget()` and the rename there used to be a window in which `getDatabase` was open to the whole process: a parallel export, a freshly created ViewModel, anything, could reopen the working file, have Room lay down fresh `-wal`/`-shm` next to it, and hold a live connection to a file about to be replaced. Holding the same monitor `getDatabase` synchronizes on closes it; nested `synchronized` on one object is reentrant, so `closeAndForget` and `checkpoint` still work inside the block.
- **`getDatabase` needs the second null check inside `synchronized`, and for a while it did not have one.** Two threads both seeing null outside would each build a Room instance over the same file; the field kept the second, and the first — with a live connection — would never be closed and `closeAndForget` would never find it.
- **The result of `wal_checkpoint` is checked.** It returns three numbers and the first is a failure flag: 1 means a reader was holding the database and the log could not be folded in. The app always has readers — every list on screen holds a Flow — so an unchecked checkpoint would hand back a copy missing the newest measurements, and the owner would find out only while restoring from it. One retry, then an outright refusal: better to say no than to pass off an incomplete copy as a whole one.
- **After a successful import the process must die.** `InventoryDatabase.closeAndForget()` drops the singleton, but the repository in `AppContainer`, every live ViewModel and every collected `Flow` still describe data that no longer exists. `SettingsScreen.restartApp` starts the launch intent with `CLEAR_TASK` and calls `Runtime.getRuntime().exit(0)`; recreating the activity would not be enough.
- **Settings do not travel with the database** — they live in `SharedPreferences` (`app_prefs` and `device_prefs`, see `:app`'s `settings/AppSettings.kt`). The database is the user's data, which they carry between phones; the reminder switch belongs to the installation, and arriving inside someone else's backup it would turn on notifications nobody asked for.
- **The database does travel in the Android backup, though, and `checkpointForBackup` is what keeps that copy whole.** `IncubatorBackupAgent` in `:app` calls it before auto backup copies the file: the same `wal_checkpoint` the export performs, for the same reason — without it the cloud gets the database as of the last checkpoint, missing the newest measurements. It differs from the export in one thing only: a busy database is not a refusal here. The export promises a complete copy and may say «try again»; a backup runs unasked, and having one slightly older beats having none.

## Deleting everything

`wipeDatabase(context)` sits next to the export and import in `DatabaseTransfer.kt` and backs «Удалить все данные» on the settings screen.

- **It deletes the file, not the rows.** `DELETE FROM` over eight tables would have to be kept in step with the schema by hand, on every migration — exactly the work the file-based transfer exists to avoid — and one forgotten table would leave live rows behind something called «удалить всё». The file knows nothing about the schema: absent means empty. Room recreates it, at the current version and with no migrations to run, on the next open.
- Everything else is the import's swap, for the import's reasons: the work happens under `InventoryDatabase.exclusively` so nothing can reopen the file between the close and the delete, and `-wal` / `-shm` go with it — a journal belonging to a deleted database will either replay foreign pages onto the fresh one or stop it opening.
- **The process must restart afterwards**, exactly as after an import, and `DataTransferController.wipe()` does it. A failure there is `TransferState.FailedNeedsRestart` rather than `Failed`: `wipeDatabase` closes the connection first, so whatever goes wrong after that, the live process can no longer work.
